Futuro · engine reference

Where every number comes from

A retirement projector is just money in three states: moving, parked, or quietly counted twice. Almost every bug in this codebase has been one of three things — one sum counted twice, two units mixed, or two different spans of time compared. This map exists so a change can be checked against the structure instead of against memory.

flowing — liq parked — iliq where it leaks
01

Who owns which number

Money enters at the left and is only ever transformed rightward. Nothing downstream may re-derive a number an upstream layer already owns — when two layers compute the same figure, one of them is wrong and you cannot tell which from the output.

02

One month, in order

Order is load-bearing. The market return is earned on the balance after asset transactions and before income and expenses land — so a sale credited one step later silently loses a month of compounding on the proceeds.

  1. 01
    Property carrying value: appreciate, or interpolate _basis → salePrice
    iliq
  2. 02
    Property sale → liq += netOfSellCosts(cv), mark sold
    liq ↑ · iliq ↓
  3. 03
    Deal asset activation — a future capital trigger fires
    iliq ↑
  4. 04
    Deal asset: appreciate, or interpolate basis → manualPrice
    iliq
  5. 05
    Deal exit → snap cv = manualPrice, liq += net, record in _preYieldExits
    liq ↑ · iliq ↓ — paired with step 11
  6. 06
    Market compounding — liq *= 1 + _liqMonthlyRate(liq, yld)
    everything above earns this month's return; everything below does not
  7. 07
    Base spend: expenseCurve[age] ?? getMonthly(age) × f, then × expMultiplier
  8. 08
    Gastos override, past months only — the actual replaces base + trips
    and suppresses items carrying a gastosCategory
  9. 09
    Itemised expenses — one-off, monthly, annual, installments, trips
    exp
  10. 10
    Incomes
    inc
  11. 11
    Deal cashflows — inc += credit − _preYieldExits[d.id]
    the subtraction is what stops step 5 counting twice
  12. 12
    Property tax on carrying value
    exp
  13. 13
    liq += inc − exp; track the minimum; push the monthly record
    liq
The start year is a partial loop It runs from the current calendar month to December, not from January. Any test asserting on a fixed early month in the start year passes until the calendar moves past it, then fails for reasons that look unrelated. Assert on December. A one-off dated earlier in the start year never fires at all — the phone flags those as already spent; the desktop shows them as ordinary future costs while equally never spending them.
03

What must stay true

Net worth conserves

Δ(liq + iliq) = inc − exp + mktGain

At a sale, liq gains exactly what iliq loses, so Δ is zero. A cliff in the curve means the carrying value was not snapped to the price.

A sold asset leaves both totals

props.filter(p => !p.sold)

In both monthly pushes and the yearly row. A frozen carrying value counted forever overstates net worth after every sale.

A budget is the whole project

future = budget − _projActual(cat)

Spread over the payments still scheduled. Counting whole payment-sized chunks discards the part-payment and spends it a second time.

Labels count the remaining run

1..N of what is left

"11 of 11" describes money already spent, at a payment size no longer being charged.

Where the same money gets counted twice

Every row is a live guard. Removing one reintroduces a bug that has already shipped once.

Two sources, one sumWhat stops the double count
A month's Gastos actual, and the itemised expenses inside it guarded items with a gastosCategory are skipped when an actual exists
Budget already spent, and future installments guarded _effInstall re-bases the money, not the payment count
A deal exit credited pre-yield, and again in the main loop guarded _preYieldExits[d.id] subtracted at step 11
A one-off before today, and liquidBase guarded past one-offs are never deducted — IBKR already reflects them
Unfiled project labour, and the project budget guarded vendor precedent routes it to the owner's own category
Elapsed time, and payments actually made guarded slotAtStart re-bases the slot to the sim's first month
A credit-card bill, and the purchases on it open both count while the bill is untagged. Tag it in Gastos
05

Two silent mismatches

Nominal versus inflated

Everything is entered in today's money. The nominal items are contractual amounts fixed at a date — and expenses and incomes must be treated identically, always.

Inflated by fLeft nominal
base spend, tripsone-off items
recurring monthly / annual expensesinstallments
recurring incomes — symmetricallydeal capital and returns

Whole plan versus the visible window

The three head tiles measure the entire plan; the chart can be clipped to five, ten or twenty years. A trough at 95 sitting under a 57–77 view is not a contradiction — but it reads as one, and was reported as a bug three times before the UI was made to say so. Tiles now print the age they refer to; the chart prints its span against the plan's.

The same mismatch runs one level down. The chart plots the monthly series, so ages are fractional and points are matched by proximity, never equality. Mixing in a yearly roll-up makes a tile disagree with the line directly beneath it.

07

Before saying a number is right

  1. Run _selfTest(). 52 of 52, no exceptions.
  2. Name the layer that owns the number. If two layers compute it, one is wrong.
  3. Walk it through the month in order. Did it land before or after the market return?
  4. Check net worth still conserves at every transaction.
  5. Look for a second path to the same money.
  6. Same units? Same span of time? Monthly or yearly?
  7. If it came from Gastos: is the month complete, and how much of it is unfiled?
